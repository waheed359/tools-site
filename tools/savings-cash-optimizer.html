Getting cash savings right is a free pay rise

Most people leave significant money on the table by keeping cash in an old account paying under 1%. Moving the same balance to today's best rates — and the right tax wrapper — can be worth hundreds a year with zero extra risk, as long as you stay inside deposit protection limits.

🇬🇧 UK cash options at a glance

  • Easy access — instant withdrawals; rates move with the Bank of England base rate.
  • Fixed-rate bonds — lock for 6 months–5 years for a known rate; no access until maturity.
  • Cash ISA — interest is tax-free for life; £20,000/year allowance (a £12,000 cash-ISA cap for under-65s is planned from April 2027).
  • Personal Savings Allowance: £1,000 of interest tax-free for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate, nil for additional-rate — the reason ISAs matter more as balances and tax bands rise.
  • Protection: FSCS covers £85,000 per person per banking licence.

🇺🇸 US cash options at a glance

  • High-yield savings (HYSA) — online banks routinely pay 10–20× the big-bank average; fully liquid.
  • Certificates of deposit (CDs) — fixed term, fixed rate, early-withdrawal penalty; good for known future expenses.
  • Treasury bills — backed by the US government and exempt from state and local income tax, which often makes them the after-tax winner in high-tax states.
  • Protection: FDIC/NCUA insures $250,000 per depositor per institution; Treasuries carry the full faith and credit of the US.

A simple structure that works

  1. Bills buffer in your current account.
  2. Emergency fund (3–6 months of essentials) in easy access / HYSA.
  3. Known future expenses in fixed bonds, CDs or T-bills maturing when needed.
  4. 5+ year money usually belongs invested, not in cash — see our ISA vs Roth optimizer.
FAQ

Common questions

Is a Cash ISA worth it if the rate is lower than a normal account?

Often yes, once your interest would exceed the Personal Savings Allowance. A basic-rate taxpayer with £30,000 at 4.3% earns £1,290 — £290 of it taxable — so a 4.1% ISA can net more. The tool does this comparison for your exact numbers.

Why do Treasury bills sometimes beat a higher bank APY?

T-bill interest is exempt from state and local income tax. In a state charging 5–10%, that exemption can outweigh a 0.2–0.5% APY gap — especially for larger balances.

Are these accounts safe?

Yes, within protection limits: FSCS covers £85,000 per UK banking licence; FDIC/NCUA cover $250,000 per US institution. Spread larger balances across providers, and note that T-bills are government-backed directly.

Fixed rate or easy access — how do I choose?

Match the lock-up to when you'll need the money. Emergency funds must stay accessible; money for a known expense in 12 months can earn the fixed-rate premium. Laddering several maturities gives you both.

How often should I review my savings rate?

At least yearly, and whenever the central bank moves rates. Loyalty is expensive — banks quietly cut rates on old accounts while advertising better ones to new customers.
