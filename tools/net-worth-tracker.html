Why net worth beats every other money metric

Income tells you what flows in; net worth tells you what stays. It's the figure that quietly decides when you can retire, how big a shock you can absorb, and whether this year's work actually made you wealthier. Calculated monthly, it also exposes problems early — lifestyle creep shows up as a flat line long before it feels like one.

What to include

  • Assets: property at realistic market value, cash, pensions/401(k)s and IRAs at current value, ISAs and brokerage accounts, vehicles at resale value, crypto, business equity.
  • Liabilities: mortgage balance, credit cards, personal and car loans, student loans, anything else owed.
  • Leave out: possessions you'd never sell (furniture, electronics) — they inflate the number without being real wealth.

Region-specific buckets this tracker uses

🇬🇧 UK savers get fields for pensions and ISAs (both grow tax-free); 🇺🇸 US savers get 401(k)/IRA and brokerage/HSA/529 buckets. Retirement money absolutely counts in net worth — it's often the largest asset people forget they own.

Benchmarks (use lightly)

  • A common rule of thumb: by 30, aim for ~1× annual salary saved across retirement accounts; ~3× by 40; ~6–8× by 55.
  • Debt-to-asset ratio under 50% is comfortable; over 80% means shocks hit hard.
  • The only benchmark that truly matters: your own number, trending up.

Your privacy

This tracker runs entirely in your browser. The optional save button writes to your device's local storage only — there is no account, no server, and no way for us (or anyone) to see your figures.

FAQ

Common questions

Where is my net worth data stored?

Only in your own browser's local storage, and only if you press Save. Nothing is transmitted or stored on any server — clearing your browser data or pressing Reset removes it completely.

Should I include my pension or 401(k) in net worth?

Yes — retirement accounts are usually among your largest assets. Some people track 'accessible net worth' (excluding retirement and home equity) separately, but the headline figure should include everything you own.

Do I count my home and mortgage?

Count the home's realistic market value as an asset and the outstanding mortgage as a liability. The difference — your equity — is what actually contributes to net worth.

How often should I update it?

Monthly is ideal: frequent enough to catch trends, infrequent enough that market noise doesn't dominate. Many people do it on payday as part of a 10-minute money routine.

Is a negative net worth bad?

It's normal early on — student loans and a new mortgage put most people below zero at some point. What matters is trajectory: paying down debt and saving moves the line up every single month.
