Snowball vs avalanche: the honest comparison

Both methods start the same way: pay every minimum, then throw every spare unit of currency at one target debt. They differ only in which debt gets the firepower:

  • ❄️ Snowball targets the smallest balance first. Each cleared debt frees its minimum payment and delivers a motivating win early.
  • 🏔️ Avalanche targets the highest interest rate first. Mathematically optimal — it always pays the least total interest.

The simulator above runs both against your actual debts, month by month, including the "rolling" of freed-up minimums — so you can see whether avalanche's saving is worth hundreds, or small enough that snowball's psychology wins.

Force multipliers worth checking first

  • 0% balance transfers (cards): moving a 25% APR balance to a 0% card (typical fee 3–4%) can shorten any plan dramatically — if you'll clear it inside the promotional window.
  • Rate renegotiation: one phone call asking for a lower APR, or a consolidation loan below your weighted average rate, beats months of grinding.
  • Minimum-payment trap: card minimums are designed to stretch repayment over decades — any fixed budget above the minimums breaks that cycle.

When to get help instead

If the budget can't cover the minimums, or debt keeps growing despite effort, free regulated help exists: 🇬🇧 StepChange, National Debtline and Citizens Advice; 🇺🇸 NFCC-member non-profit credit counselling agencies. Debt management plans and other formal options can freeze interest — far better than borrowing more to cope.

FAQ

Common questions

Which is better, snowball or avalanche?

Avalanche always wins mathematically — it minimises total interest. But studies of real borrowers show snowball's early wins improve follow-through. The simulator shows the exact cost of choosing snowball for your debts; if it's small, pick the one you'll stick to.

Should I include my mortgage or student loan?

Usually no. These strategies are for expensive, unsecured debt — cards, personal loans, car finance. Mortgages and income-contingent UK student loans have their own economics (see our dedicated planners).

What if I can only pay the minimums?

Then balances barely move and card debt can grow. Prioritise raising the budget even slightly, check 0% transfer options, and if minimums are unaffordable, contact a free debt charity — formal plans can freeze interest.

Do extra one-off payments help?

Enormously — a lump sum aimed at the target debt skips months of the plan. Windfalls, tax refunds and bonus months are rocket fuel for either strategy.

Will paying off debt improve my credit score?

Generally yes: lower credit utilisation and cleared accounts both help over time. Expect small temporary dips when accounts close; the long-term direction is clearly positive.
