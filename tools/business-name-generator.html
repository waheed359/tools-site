What makes a business name actually work

Forget "clever" — the names that build brands share four boring, measurable traits:

  • Easy to say and spell — if people can't type it after hearing it once, you'll bleed traffic to misspellings forever.
  • Distinctive in your market — it can't be confused with a competitor (that's also the legal test for trademarks).
  • Ownable — the domain, trademark class and social handles are available at sane prices.
  • Room to grow — "Leeds Laptop Repair" is great SEO until you expand to phones, or Manchester.

The six naming styles this generator uses

  1. Compounds (keyword + industry word): Stripe-era clarity — Brewstack, Pixelworks.
  2. Modern brandables (suffix-modified): Bloomly, Pixelio — short, domain-friendly.
  3. Classic (& Co., Prefix + Name): instant trust for finance, law, trades.
  4. Descriptive: tells customers exactly what you do — strong for local SEO.
  5. Evocative (Latin-ish pairings): premium feel — Nova Brew, Terra Form.
  6. Playful: memorable for consumer brands — use with care in serious industries.

The validation checklist (do it in this order)

  1. Domain: check the .com first — even UK businesses benefit from owning it. The 🌐 link searches availability instantly.
  2. Trademark: search 🇺🇸 USPTO / 🇬🇧 UKIPO for identical and similar marks in your class. A registered conflict means rebranding later, at your cost.
  3. Company register: 🇬🇧 Companies House name availability; 🇺🇸 your state's Secretary of State business search.
  4. Social handles & Google: consistent handles matter, and page one of Google for the exact name shouldn't be dominated by someone else.

Shortlist three candidates that pass everything, sleep on it, say each aloud on an imaginary phone call — then commit and register the domain the same day.

FAQ

Common questions

Does this check if the name is actually available?

It generates candidates locally and gives you one-click links to the authoritative sources — domain search, USPTO/UKIPO trademark databases, and Companies House. Availability changes by the minute, so checking the live registers directly is the only reliable method.

Do I need to trademark my business name?

You can trade without one, but registration (UKIPO ~£170+, USPTO ~$250–350 per class) gives you exclusive rights in your class and the power to stop copycats. At minimum, search the registers first so you don't infringe someone else's mark.

What if the .com is taken?

If it's parked and affordable, consider buying it. Otherwise a modifier (get-, try-, hq) or a strong alternative TLD (.co.uk for UK businesses, .io/.co for tech) can work — but avoid names where an active competitor owns the .com.

Can two businesses have the same name?

Yes, if they're in different industries or regions and no trademark conflict exists — think Delta Airlines and Delta Faucets. The legal test is likelihood of customer confusion in the same class of goods or services.

Should my name describe what I do?

Descriptive names win local search ('Austin Deck Builders') but are hard to trademark and limit expansion. Brandable names (Stripe, Monzo) are protectable and flexible but need marketing to attach meaning. Many businesses pair both: a brandable name plus a descriptive tagline.
