Two countries, two completely different loans

The biggest student-loan mistake is applying one country's logic to the other. A UK student loan is income-contingent: repayments depend only on earnings, stop when you stop earning, and the balance is wiped after 25–40 years. A US student loan is a genuine debt: interest compounds until you clear it, and the balance follows you until it's paid (or forgiven under specific programs).

🇺🇸 US repayment levers

  • Extra principal payments — the most reliable saver; even $50–100/month removes years and thousands of interest.
  • Income-driven repayment (IDR) — caps federal payments at a share of discretionary income, with forgiveness after 20–25 years (taxable in most cases).
  • PSLF — tax-free forgiveness after 120 qualifying payments in public-service employment.
  • Refinancing — can cut private-market rates, but converts federal loans into private ones, permanently losing IDR, deferment and forgiveness options.
FAQ

Common questions

Should I overpay my UK student loan?

Only if you'll clear it before write-off — typically high earners with smaller balances. If the simulator shows a write-off, the loan is effectively a 9% graduate tax and overpayments mostly reduce the amount that would have been wiped anyway.

What are the UK repayment thresholds for 2026/27?

Plan 1: £26,900; Plan 2: £29,385; Plan 4: £33,795; Plan 5: £25,000; Postgraduate: £21,000. You repay 9% (6% postgraduate) of income above your threshold, deducted through payroll.

Do UK student loans affect my credit score or mortgage?

They don't appear on your credit file, but lenders count the monthly deduction in affordability calculations, which can modestly reduce how much you can borrow.

Is refinancing US federal loans a good idea?

Only when you're confident you won't need federal protections. Refinancing can lower the rate for strong credit, but you permanently lose income-driven plans, generous deferment and all forgiveness routes, including PSLF.

How do extra payments save so much interest?

Every extra dollar goes straight to principal, so all future interest is charged on a smaller balance. The earlier in the loan you overpay, the larger the compounding saving — the calculator shows your exact figure.
