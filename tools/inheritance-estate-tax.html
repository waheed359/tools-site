Inheritance and estate tax in plain English

Both countries tax wealth at death, but the thresholds are worlds apart: the UK starts charging at £325,000; the US federal system doesn't bite until $15 million (2026). That difference shapes completely different planning strategies.

🇬🇧 UK Inheritance Tax (IHT)

  • Nil-rate band: £325,000 tax-free per person, frozen for years.
  • Residence nil-rate band: an extra £175,000 when the home passes to children or grandchildren (tapered away above £2m estates).
  • Spouse exemption: transfers between spouses are unlimited and tax-free, and unused bands transfer — a couple can pass up to £1 million before IHT.
  • Rate: 40% above the bands (36% if 10%+ of the estate goes to charity).
  • The 7-year rule: gifts survive IHT entirely if you live 7 years; taper relief reduces the tax on gifts made 3–7 years before death. Annual exemption: £3,000/year of gifts immediately outside the estate.

🇺🇸 US estate tax

  • Federal exemption: $15,000,000 per person for 2026 (made permanent at this level, inflation-indexed), with portability letting a surviving spouse use the deceased spouse's unused exemption.
  • Rate: up to 40% above the exemption; unlimited marital deduction for US-citizen spouses.
  • Annual gifting: $19,000 per recipient per year (2026) passes completely tax-free without touching the exemption.
  • State taxes are the real trap: around 17 states levy estate or inheritance taxes with much lower thresholds — Oregon from $1M, Massachusetts from $2M, and six states tax inheritors directly.

Planning moves that legitimately reduce the bill

  • Use spouse exemptions and band transfers fully — often the single biggest saver.
  • Gift early and document it: the 7-year clock (UK) and annual exclusions (US) only work if started in time.
  • Pensions, life insurance written in trust (UK) and irrevocable trusts (US) can sit outside the taxable estate.
  • Charitable gifts reduce both the taxable estate and, in the UK, potentially the rate itself.
FAQ

Common questions

How much can a UK couple leave tax-free?

Up to £1,000,000: two £325,000 nil-rate bands plus two £175,000 residence bands, provided the home passes to direct descendants and the estate stays under the £2m taper threshold.

What is the 7-year rule?

UK gifts made more than 7 years before death escape IHT completely. Gifts within 7 years use up the nil-rate band first; if tax is due on a gift, taper relief reduces it on gifts made 3–7 years before death.

Who actually pays US estate tax?

Very few estates — only those above the $15M (2026) federal exemption, roughly 0.1% of deaths. State-level estate and inheritance taxes catch far more families, with thresholds as low as $1M.

Do beneficiaries pay tax on what they inherit?

In the UK and under US federal law, the estate pays, not the recipient. Six US states, however, levy an inheritance tax on beneficiaries (spouses and often children exempt). Inherited US assets also get a step-up in cost basis for capital gains.

Is this calculator a substitute for estate planning advice?

No — it's a planning estimate using headline rules. Trusts, business/agricultural reliefs, foreign assets and non-citizen spouses all change the answer materially; use the result to have a better-informed conversation with a professional.
