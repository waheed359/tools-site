Why landlord tax is where deals are won and lost

Two landlords with identical properties can keep very different profits purely because of structure. The UK's Section 24 rules and the US depreciation system pull in opposite directions — one punishes leverage for individuals, the other rewards it.

🇬🇧 Section 24: the interest restriction

  • Since 2020, individual landlords cannot deduct mortgage interest from rental income. Instead they receive a flat 20% tax credit on the interest.
  • Basic-rate taxpayers are broadly unaffected; higher and additional-rate landlords are taxed on revenue they never received — effective tax rates on real profit can exceed 60%, or turn a cash profit into an after-tax loss.
  • Limited companies escape Section 24: interest is fully deductible and profits pay corporation tax (19% up to £50k, 25% above with marginal relief). The trade-offs: dividend tax on extraction, pricier mortgages, accounts and filing costs, and CGT + SDLT on moving existing properties in.
  • Rule of thumb the calculator makes concrete: higher-rate taxpayers with meaningful leverage usually benefit from a company for new purchases; basic-rate, low-leverage landlords usually don't.

🇺🇸 Schedule E and the depreciation machine

  • Rentals report on Schedule E: rent minus operating expenses minus fully deductible mortgage interest.
  • Then comes depreciation — the building's cost (excluding land) written off over 27.5 years. It's a non-cash deduction that frequently wipes out the taxable income on a cash-flowing property.
  • Paper losses can offset other income up to $25,000/year for active participants (phasing out between $100k–$150k MAGI); excess losses carry forward.
  • The deferred bill: depreciation is recaptured at up to 25% on sale, unless rolled into a 1031 exchange.

Before you decide structure

Model the full journey — purchase costs, annual tax (this tool), extraction or sale taxes — not just year one. And remember the comparison here shows retained company profits; your plans for the money change the answer.

FAQ

Common questions

What is Section 24?

The UK rule (fully in force since April 2020) that stops individual landlords deducting mortgage interest from rental income, replacing it with a 20% tax credit. It mainly hurts higher-rate taxpayers with leveraged properties — exactly what the personal column of this calculator shows.

Should I buy through a limited company?

Often yes for new purchases by higher-rate taxpayers with mortgages, because interest stays deductible and corporation tax starts at 19%. Often no for basic-rate or unleveraged landlords once dividend tax, dearer mortgages and admin are counted. Moving existing properties in triggers CGT and stamp duty — get advice first.

How does rental depreciation work in the US?

Residential buildings depreciate straight-line over 27.5 years — purchase price minus land value, divided by 27.5, deducted every year regardless of cash spent. It's why profitable rentals frequently show little or no taxable income.

Can rental losses reduce my salary tax?

US: up to $25,000 of passive rental losses can offset other income if you actively participate and MAGI is under $100,000 (phasing out to $150,000); the rest carries forward. UK: rental losses only carry forward against future rental profits.

What happens to depreciation when I sell?

The IRS recaptures it: accumulated depreciation is taxed at up to 25% on sale, separate from capital gains. A 1031 like-kind exchange defers both; holding until death currently erases them via the step-up in basis.
