Why a weekly plan saves real money (not just time)

Households that plan meals consistently spend less on food through three mechanisms: fewer impulse takeaways (the 6pm "nothing's defrosted" tax), one targeted shop instead of several top-ups, and less waste — the average family bins a significant share of the food it buys, almost all of it unplanned purchases. A written list alone measurably cuts supermarket spend.

How this planner builds your week

  • Four diet styles — balanced, vegetarian, high-protein and budget — each with a pool of straightforward dinners using supermarket-standard ingredients.
  • Time filter — weeknight-realistic 30-minute meals, or up to an hour if you enjoy cooking.
  • Scaled quantities — every ingredient multiplies by your household size, then merges into one de-duplicated list.
  • Region-aware units — metric (g/ml) for 🇬🇧 UK shoppers, imperial (oz/fl oz) for 🇺🇸 US shoppers, switched by the header toggle.
  • Deliberate overlap — meals share ingredients (onions, rice, tomatoes, eggs) so packs get finished, not binned.

Make the plan stick

  1. Shop once, early. Order online or go Sunday morning with the copied list — decisions made calmly are cheaper than decisions made hungry.
  2. Prep the two worst nights. Chop or batch for your busiest evenings; those are the nights takeaways win.
  3. Keep a "panic shelf". One pasta-and-pesto style backup meal stops a single chaotic day derailing the week.
  4. Reuse the shuffle. A fresh combination each week prevents the boredom that kills most meal plans by week three.
FAQ

Common questions

Are the cost estimates accurate?

They're based on typical mainstream-supermarket prices (Tesco/Sainsbury's/Aldi in the UK; Walmart/Kroger/Aldi in the US) and scale with your household size. Brands, region and offers move real costs around — treat them as a solid planning baseline.

Can I swap meals between days?

Of course — the days are just a suggested order. The grocery list covers all seven dinners regardless of sequence, and the Shuffle button rebuilds the whole week if you want different meals.

Does the plan cover breakfast and lunch?

It focuses on dinners — the meal where planning saves the most money and stress. Most households repeat simple breakfasts and lunches; add those staples to the copied list before shopping.

How do I get the list onto my phone?

Tap 'Copy grocery list' and paste it into your notes or shopping app, or use Print to save the whole plan as a PDF. The list is plain text, so it pastes cleanly anywhere.

Are the portions nutritionally balanced?

Each dinner follows the familiar protein + carbohydrate + vegetables structure with sensible per-person portions. For specific dietary or medical needs, adjust quantities or consult a professional — this is a planning tool, not dietary advice.
