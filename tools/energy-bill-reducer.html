Where home energy money actually goes

In a typical home, heating (and cooling) is 50–60% of the bill, hot water ~15%, appliances ~15%, lighting and electronics the rest. That's why thermostat, insulation and heating-control changes dominate any honest savings ranking, while unplugging phone chargers saves pennies. This tool applies those proportions to your bill so the ranking reflects your home, not an average.

The hierarchy of savings

  1. Free, instant: tariff/plan switching (if you haven't in 2+ years, you're almost certainly overpaying), thermostat down 1–2°, boiler flow temperature to ~60°C, cold washes, air-drying.
  2. Cheap, fast payback: draught-proofing, LED swaps, smart plugs, TRVs and heating schedules — mostly under £150/$180 with payback inside a year or two.
  3. Investment, big payback: loft/attic insulation top-ups, cavity/wall insulation, and ultimately a heat pump — where 🇬🇧 the £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant and 🇺🇸 the 30% federal tax credit (up to $2,000) plus utility rebates change the economics entirely.

Support worth checking before you spend

  • 🇬🇧 ECO4 and the Great British Insulation Scheme fund insulation for eligible households; the Warm Home Discount gives £150 off electricity bills for qualifying homes.
  • 🇺🇸 Most utilities offer free or cheap energy audits, and Inflation Reduction Act programs rebate insulation, air sealing and heat pumps — stacking with the federal tax credits.

Two habits that keep bills down for good

Read your meter (or smart-meter app) monthly — usage you watch, you manage; and diary your tariff end date — the single most expensive energy habit in both countries is rolling silently onto a default rate.

FAQ

Common questions

How accurate are the savings estimates?

They apply well-documented typical percentages (e.g. ~6–10% heating saving per degree, ~10% from switching) to your actual bill. Treat each figure as a realistic range for a typical home — your insulation, occupancy and climate shift the exact numbers.

What saves the most money fastest?

For most households: switching tariff/plan if you haven't recently, then thermostat and heating-schedule changes. They're free, immediate, and attack the 50–60% of the bill that heating represents.

Is a heat pump worth it?

Increasingly yes. Heat pumps deliver 3–4 units of heat per unit of electricity; with the UK's £7,500 grant or the US 30% tax credit plus rebates, running costs can beat gas — especially replacing oil or electric-resistance heating. Get a proper heat-loss survey first.

Does turning the heating off and on cost more than leaving it on low?

Heating only when needed uses less energy than heating continuously — the 'leave it on low' myth persists because poorly insulated homes feel cold quickly. Timers/smart thermostats give you comfort and the saving.

Why does the tool ask about my tumble dryer?

It's typically the single hungriest appliance after heating and hot water — heavy use can add £100–£200/$120–$250 a year, which is why halving it ranks above most gadget-level tips.
