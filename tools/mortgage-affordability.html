How mortgage affordability is really decided

Lenders don't start from the house you want — they start from your income, your existing debts and the risk that rates rise. This calculator mirrors the two frameworks underwriters actually use.

🇬🇧 UK: income multiples + stress test

  • 4.5× income cap: regulators limit the share of lending above 4.5× income, so most borrowers are offered up to 4.5× their (joint) salary, reduced by committed debts.
  • Stress testing: lenders check you could still afford payments if your rate rose around 3 percentage points.
  • Loan-to-value (LTV): the best rates sit at 60% LTV; 90–95% LTV deals exist but cost more. A bigger deposit buys a cheaper rate and a bigger budget.
  • Stamp duty: budget for SDLT on top of the price — the calculator shows both the standard and first-time-buyer figures (first-time buyers pay nothing up to £300,000).

🇺🇸 USA: the 28/36 DTI rule

  • Front-end ratio: housing costs (principal, interest, taxes, insurance — PITI) should stay under 28% of gross monthly income.
  • Back-end ratio: all debt payments combined should stay under 36% for conventional loans; FHA routinely allows 43% and sometimes more.
  • Down payment: 20% avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI); conventional loans start at 3–5% down and FHA at 3.5%.
  • Closing costs: keep an extra 2–5% of the price aside for fees, escrow and title.

Tips to increase what you can borrow

  • Clear or reduce monthly debts before applying — £200/$200 of monthly payments can cut your budget by tens of thousands.
  • Extend the term to lower the monthly payment (you'll pay more interest overall, but you can overpay later).
  • Check your credit report months ahead; errors and missed payments directly limit the multiple or rate you're offered.
FAQ

Common questions

How many times my salary can I borrow for a UK mortgage?

Most lenders offer up to 4.5× your single or joint income, minus adjustments for debts and dependants. A minority of lenders stretch to 5× or 5.5× for higher earners or certain professions.

What is the 28/36 rule for US mortgages?

A guideline used by underwriters: spend no more than 28% of gross monthly income on housing (PITI) and no more than 36% on all debt payments combined. FHA loans allow higher ratios, often up to 43%.

How much deposit or down payment do I need?

UK mortgages generally start at 5% deposit (95% LTV), with much better rates at 10–25%. In the US, conventional loans start around 3–5% down and FHA at 3.5%, but 20% down avoids PMI.

Does this calculator include stamp duty or closing costs?

The UK version shows estimated SDLT for both home movers and first-time buyers on your maximum price. For the US, plan for closing costs of roughly 2–5% of the purchase price on top of your down payment.

Is this a mortgage offer or pre-approval?

No — it's an educational estimate using standard underwriting rules. Your actual offer depends on credit history, the property and the individual lender, so get an agreement in principle / pre-approval before making offers.
