What this contract generator creates

Three of the most-used agreements in freelance and small-business life, assembled from professionally structured clauses with the details you enter:

  • Mutual NDA — definition of confidential information, obligations, standard exclusions, return/destruction, and injunctive-relief wording.
  • Freelance / independent contractor agreement — services, payment with statutory late-payment interest, contractor-status language (🇬🇧 including IR35-aware substitution and control wording; 🇺🇸 including tax-responsibility clauses), IP assignment on payment, and clean termination terms.
  • Service agreement — the client-side counterpart: scope, charges plus VAT/sales tax, client obligations, liability caps and termination for convenience.

Why jurisdiction wording matters

A contract's governing-law clause decides whose rules interpret it and whose courts hear disputes. 🇬🇧 England & Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland are separate legal systems — a Scottish counterparty under an "England and Wales" clause can create real friction. 🇺🇸 Each state has its own contract law; Delaware and New York are common commercial choices, but the sensible default for small engagements is usually where you (or the client) are based.

Clauses people most often get wrong

  • IP that transfers before payment — the template assigns deliverables only on payment in full, your best leverage against late payers.
  • No late-payment interest — UK freelancers can charge statutory interest (8% + base) plus fixed compensation under the 1998 Act; the clause activates that right.
  • Unlimited liability — the cap at fees paid is a standard, fair protection both sides usually accept.
  • Vague scope — the single biggest source of disputes. Be specific in the scope field; "website design" invites scope creep, "design of a 5-page marketing site per the brief dated…" doesn't.

Important: this generator produces a solid starting template, not legal advice. For high-value, regulated or unusual deals, have a qualified lawyer review the final text.

FAQ

Common questions

Is a generated contract legally binding?

A contract is binding when there's offer, acceptance, consideration and intent — which a signed version of this template can satisfy. The generator gives you professional structure; the binding force comes from both parties signing it. Have significant agreements reviewed by a lawyer.

Do NDAs really work?

Yes — a well-drafted NDA creates enforceable confidentiality duties and supports injunctions and damages for breach. Its practical power is also preventative: parties take care with information they've formally promised to protect.

What is the IR35 wording in the freelance contract for?

UK contractors engaged through intermediaries are taxed as employees if the engagement looks like employment. The template includes genuine-business indicators — control over how work is done and a right of substitution — but your actual working practices matter as much as the words.

Can I edit the generated text?

Absolutely — that's the point. Copy it into your document editor, adjust clauses to the deal, and delete anything that doesn't apply. The structure and numbering make edits easy.

Is my information stored when I generate a contract?

No. The document is assembled entirely in your browser; names, fees and scope are never transmitted or saved anywhere.
