Pension transfers and rollovers: the decisions you can't undo

Whether it's a UK final-salary scheme or an old American 401(k), moving retirement money involves trading guarantees, fees and flexibility against each other. The right answer depends on what you'd be giving up — which is why this tool asks before it answers.

🇬🇧 UK: the £30,000 rule and DB safeguards

  • Defined Benefit (final salary) pensions pay a guaranteed income for life, usually inflation-linked with a spouse's pension. By law, transfers of safeguarded benefits worth over £30,000 require advice from an FCA-regulated pension transfer specialist.
  • The FCA's starting assumption is that a DB transfer is unsuitable for most people — the guarantee is typically worth more than the cash offered.
  • Defined Contribution transfers are far simpler: the main wins are lower fees, better funds and consolidation. Check for exit penalties, guaranteed annuity rates and protected tax-free cash first.

🇺🇸 US: the four 401(k) routes

  1. Direct rollover to an IRA — maximum investment choice, usually lowest cost. No tax if done trustee-to-trustee.
  2. Roll into the new employer's 401(k) — simple, strong legal protection, preserves the Rule of 55 and keeps the backdoor-Roth route clean.
  3. Leave it in the old plan — fine if fees are low and the balance is above the $7,000 force-out threshold.
  4. Cash out — income tax plus a 10% penalty before 59½, and the permanent loss of decades of compounding. Rarely rational.

Red flags that mean stop

  • Anyone who contacted you first about transferring — UK pension cold-calling is illegal.
  • Pressure to decide before a "deadline", overseas investment schemes, or promised returns above ~8%.
  • A rollover check made payable to you personally rather than to the new custodian.
FAQ

Common questions

Do I legally need advice to transfer my UK pension?

Only for safeguarded (defined benefit / guaranteed) rights worth more than £30,000 — then FCA-regulated advice is mandatory and schemes will not process the transfer without it. Ordinary defined-contribution transfers don't require advice, though it can still be sensible.

What's the difference between a direct and indirect 401(k) rollover?

A direct rollover sends money straight between providers with no tax. An indirect rollover pays you personally, triggers 20% mandatory withholding, and gives you just 60 days to redeposit the full amount — miss it and the whole sum becomes taxable plus penalty. Always choose direct.

How much does cashing out a 401(k) early really cost?

You pay ordinary income tax on the whole balance plus a 10% penalty if you're under 59½ — often 30%+ gone immediately. The bigger cost is losing decades of compounding; the tool shows what the same balance could become by 65.

Will transferring lose my employer contributions?

Vested contributions are yours and transfer with the pot. Unvested employer money is forfeited if you leave before the vesting schedule completes — check your plan documents before timing a job move.

How long does a pension transfer take?

UK DC transfers via electronic systems typically complete in 2–4 weeks; DB transfers take several months because of the advice process. US direct rollovers usually take 2–6 weeks depending on the old custodian.
