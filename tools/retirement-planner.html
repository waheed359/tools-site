How much do you actually need to retire?

A widely-used starting point is the 4% rule: a diversified pot can typically pay out about 4% of its starting value each year, adjusted for inflation, with a good chance of lasting 30 years. Flip it around and you get the 25× rule — multiply your target annual income by 25 to find the pot you need. Want £30,000 a year? Aim for roughly £750,000 (less whatever the State Pension or Social Security covers).

🇬🇧 UK pension essentials (2026/27)

  • Auto-enrolment minimums: 8% of qualifying earnings in total — at least 3% from your employer.
  • Tax relief: contributions get 20–45% tax relief; the annual allowance is £60,000.
  • Tax-free cash: from age 55 (57 from 2028) you can usually take 25% of the pot tax-free.
  • State Pension: the full new State Pension adds roughly £12,100 a year on top if you have 35 qualifying years.

🇺🇸 US retirement essentials (2026)

  • 401(k): contribute up to $24,500 ($32,500 if 50+). Always capture the full employer match — it's an instant 50–100% return.
  • IRA: an extra $7,500 ($8,600 if 50+), traditional or Roth depending on income and tax strategy.
  • Social Security: the average retired-worker benefit is around $24,000/yr — a floor, not a plan.

The three levers that matter most

  1. Start date: thanks to compounding, £200/month from age 25 typically beats £400/month from 40.
  2. Contribution rate: each extra 1% of salary is barely noticeable now but enormous over 30 years.
  3. Fees: a 1% annual fee difference can consume roughly a fifth of a lifetime pot — check what you're paying.
FAQ

Common questions

What growth rate should I assume?

A diversified global equity portfolio has historically returned around 5–8% a year nominal over long periods. Many planners use 5–6% to stay conservative; the calculator lets you test any figure, and lower is safer for planning.

What is the 4% rule?

A rule of thumb from retirement research: withdrawing 4% of your pot in year one, then adjusting for inflation, has historically lasted about 30 years with a balanced portfolio. It's a planning guide, not a guarantee.

Does the projection include the State Pension or Social Security?

The pot projection is your private savings only. The UK results note the full new State Pension (~£12,100/yr in 2026/27) and the US section reminds you of average Social Security benefits — add those on top of the 4% income.

Should I contribute to a pension/401(k) or pay off debt first?

Always capture any employer match first — it's free money. Beyond the match, compare your debt interest rate with expected investment growth: clearing debt above ~6–7% interest usually beats investing.

Is my money locked away until retirement?

Mostly yes. UK pensions are accessible from 55 (57 from 2028). US 401(k)/IRA withdrawals before 59½ generally face a 10% penalty plus tax, with limited exceptions — that lock-in is part of why the tax breaks exist.
