What makes an invoice professional (and legally proper)

An invoice is more than a payment request — it's a tax document. Get the required fields right and you get paid faster, pass audits, and stay compliant:

  • Unique sequential number — required for UK VAT invoices and best practice everywhere; it's how both sides' bookkeeping reconciles.
  • Full names and addresses of supplier and customer.
  • Date and payment terms — "Due within 14 days" starts a clock; no terms means statutory defaults apply (30 days in the UK for B2B).
  • Itemised lines — description, quantity, unit price, amount. Vague single lines invite queries and delays.
  • Tax handled correctly — see below.
  • Payment details — bank details and a reference, so paying you is friction-free.

🇬🇧 VAT on UK invoices

  • Only charge VAT if you're VAT-registered (compulsory above £90,000 taxable turnover). Registered businesses must show their VAT number and the VAT amount separately — this generator does both.
  • Standard rate is 20%; some supplies are reduced-rate (5%) or zero-rated.
  • Unpaid on time? UK businesses can add statutory interest (8% + Bank rate) plus fixed compensation of £40–£100 per invoice under the Late Payment Act — the generated invoice reserves that right.

🇺🇸 Sales tax on US invoices

  • Sales tax is state/local, typically 0–10%, and many services are exempt in many states — hence the adjustable rate (leave 0 if not applicable).
  • If you have nexus and taxable sales, show the tax as a separate line, as the generator does.

Getting paid faster

Invoices sent the day work completes are paid weeks earlier on average. Short terms (7–14 days), a named contact, and bank details on the invoice all measurably reduce payment time — and a numbered PDF (use the Print button → Save as PDF) looks like a business that chases its money.

FAQ

Common questions

How do I save the invoice as a PDF?

Click Print / Save PDF and choose 'Save as PDF' as the destination in your browser's print dialog. The page strips the website styling so only the clean invoice prints.

Do I need to charge VAT on my invoice?

Only if you're VAT-registered (mandatory once taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 in the UK). Unregistered businesses select the 0% option — the invoice then states that no VAT is charged.

What should my invoice number look like?

Any unique, sequential scheme works: INV-001, 2026-014, or client-prefixed like ACME-007. Never reuse or skip numbers randomly — sequence gaps raise questions in VAT and tax inspections.

Can I add my logo?

After generating, use Print → Save as PDF and add the PDF to your letterhead, or copy the invoice into a document editor and drop your logo in the header — the layout is built to survive pasting.

Is my client's information stored?

No. The invoice is assembled entirely in your browser; nothing you type is transmitted or saved. Refreshing the page clears it, so save your PDF first.
