Why a simple will matters more than people think

Die without a will and intestacy rules decide everything: unmarried partners typically inherit nothing, stepchildren are excluded, and courts choose your children's guardians. A simple, properly witnessed will fixes all of that in a few pages — and the structure is remarkably standard, which is what this drafter builds.

The anatomy of a simple will

  1. Revocation — cancels all earlier wills so only this one speaks.
  2. Executors — the people who administer the estate. Appoint two, or one plus a substitute.
  3. Guardians — who raises minor children; the clause courts give great weight to.
  4. Specific gifts — fixed sums or named items, with a 30-day survivorship condition to avoid double probate.
  5. Residuary estate — everything else; the clause that actually distributes most wealth, with a backup beneficiary.
  6. Attestation — the signing block, where most home-made wills fail.

Three different signing regimes — the drafter picks the right one

  • 🇬🇧 England & Wales: signed before two witnesses present at the same time, who then sign. A witness (or their spouse) who is also a beneficiary loses their gift.
  • 🏴 Scotland: a distinct system — sign every page before one witness to make the will self-proving; "legal rights" also guarantee spouses and children a share of moveable estate regardless of the will.
  • 🇺🇸 United States: two disinterested witnesses in almost every state, with an optional notarised self-proving affidavit that lets probate accept the will without tracking witnesses down. Community-property states (CA, TX, AZ…) limit what one spouse can give away alone.

When a template isn't enough

Use a solicitor or attorney for: estates near inheritance/estate-tax thresholds, business or farm assets, trusts for vulnerable beneficiaries, deliberately excluding a close family member, foreign property, or blended-family arrangements. A professional will costs far less than the dispute it prevents.

FAQ

Common questions

Is a will made from a template valid?

Yes, if it's properly signed and witnessed for your jurisdiction — validity comes from the formalities, not from who typed it. The drafter prints the exact witnessing rules under the signature block. Complex estates still warrant professional drafting.

Who should I choose as executor?

Someone organised, trustworthy and likely to outlive you — commonly a spouse, adult child, sibling or close friend. Executors can also be beneficiaries. Naming two (or a substitute) protects against one being unable to act.

Can a beneficiary witness my will?

In England & Wales a gift to a witness or their spouse is void (the will survives, the gift doesn't). US states have similar 'interested witness' rules. Always use witnesses who inherit nothing.

What happens to the will after signing?

Store the signed original somewhere safe — with a solicitor, a will-storage service, or a fireproof box — and tell your executors where it is. A will nobody can find is as useless as no will.

How often should I update my will?

Review it after marriage (which revokes a will in England & Wales), divorce, children, significant asset changes, or a beneficiary's death — and otherwise every 3–5 years. Small changes can be made by codicil; bigger ones deserve a fresh will.
