🇺🇸 US transfer taxes and closing costs

  • No federal tax — each state (and often county/city) sets its own, from 0% to roughly 4%.
  • Examples: Texas and a dozen others charge nothing; Washington is graduated 1.1–3%; Delaware charges 4%; New York adds a "mansion tax" from $1M.
  • Who pays is negotiable — local custom assigns it to buyer or seller, but contracts can shift it.
  • Closing costs (lender fees, title insurance, escrow, appraisal, recording) typically add 2–5% of the price for buyers.

Budgeting tips

  • SDLT/transfer tax is cash due at completion — it can't usually be added to the mortgage, so include it in your deposit planning.
  • UK buyers replacing a main home who temporarily own two properties pay the 5% surcharge but can reclaim it if the old home sells within 3 years.
  • US buyers: compare Loan Estimates from 2–3 lenders — title and lender fees vary enough to save four figures.
FAQ

Common questions

How much stamp duty does a first-time buyer pay?

Nothing up to £300,000, then 5% on the portion between £300,000 and £500,000. Buy above £500,000 and the relief vanishes completely — you pay standard home-mover rates on the whole price.

What is the additional property surcharge?

An extra 5% of the entire purchase price on second homes and buy-to-lets in England & NI, charged on top of the standard bands. It applies even if the second property is abroad or inherited part-shares exist, with limited exceptions.

When is SDLT paid?

Within 14 days of completion. In practice your conveyancer collects it with your completion funds and files the return — but the money must be available as cash, not mortgage.

Which US states have no transfer tax?

Around a dozen, including Texas, Missouri, Indiana, Mississippi, New Mexico, North Dakota, Wyoming, Montana, Oregon (most counties), Alaska, Idaho and Utah — though recording fees still apply everywhere.

Can stamp duty or transfer tax be added to the mortgage?

UK: no — SDLT must be paid in cash at completion (borrowing more against the property to cover it reduces your effective deposit). US: closing costs can sometimes be rolled into the loan or covered by seller credits, subject to lender rules.
