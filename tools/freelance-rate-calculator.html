Why freelancers systematically undercharge

An employee on £40,000 doesn't cost their employer £40,000 — pensions, National Insurance, holiday, sick pay, equipment and downtime push the real cost far higher. When you freelance, you fund all of that. Dividing a salary by 260 working days produces a rate that quietly pays you much less than the job you left.

The four leaks between your rate and your pocket

  1. Unbillable time. Marketing, proposals, invoicing, admin and learning typically consume 20–40% of working hours. Billing 4 days out of 5 is a healthy practice, not a failure.
  2. Time off. 5–6 weeks of holiday, bank holidays and sickness come straight out of billable capacity — nobody pays a freelancer for August on the beach.
  3. Expenses. Software, insurance, hardware, accounting and co-working easily total thousands a year, all paid from gross revenue.
  4. Tax. 🇬🇧 UK sole traders pay Income Tax plus Class 4 NICs (6% on profits £12,570–£50,270, 2% above) via Self Assessment. 🇺🇸 US freelancers pay 15.3% self-employment tax on 92.35% of net profit plus federal (and usually state) income tax, with quarterly estimated payments due April, June, September and January.

From minimum rate to market rate

This calculator outputs your floor — the rate below which freelancing pays worse than the equivalent job. Your market rate should sit above it, reflecting specialisation, outcomes delivered and demand. Common practice: round up to a clean number, add 20–30% margin, and raise rates with every second or third new client.

🇬🇧 Contractors working through intermediaries should also check their IR35 status — inside-IR35 engagements are taxed like employment, which materially raises the rate needed to hit the same take-home.

FAQ

Common questions

How many billable days should I assume per year?

A common planning figure is 180–200. Start from 52 weeks, subtract 5–6 weeks off, then count only the days you'll invoice — most established freelancers bill 3–4 days out of each working week once admin and sales are accounted for.

What taxes do UK freelancers pay?

Sole traders pay Income Tax on profits plus Class 4 National Insurance (6% between £12,570 and £50,270, 2% above) through Self Assessment, due 31 January with payments on account. The first £1,000 of trading income is covered by the trading allowance.

What is US self-employment tax?

15.3% (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) charged on 92.35% of net self-employment profit, on top of regular income tax. Half of it is deductible, and you prepay via quarterly estimated taxes.

Should I charge a day rate or hourly rate?

Day (or project) rates generally earn more and are easier to sell for skilled work — clients buy outcomes, not minutes. Hourly suits small, unpredictable tasks. This tool gives you both from the same target.

Does the calculator account for IR35 or incorporation?

It models a sole trader / 1099 freelancer, which is the right baseline for setting rates. Limited-company and inside-IR35 structures change the tax mechanics — treat the result as your floor and take advice on structure separately.
